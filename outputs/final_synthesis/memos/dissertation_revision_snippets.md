# Dissertation Revision Snippets

## 1. Abstract Caveat Sentence

These results should be interpreted as evidence on firms validated under the SBTi rule architecture prevailing through the 2012-2024 sample period; later SBTi changes to finance-sector standards, FLAG guidance, Scope 3 treatment, target review cycles, and status reporting do not retroactively invalidate the findings but do require caution when generalising them to the post-2025 standards environment.

## 2. Data/Methodology Caveat On SBTi Rule Regime

The empirical design uses each firm's first validated near-term SBTi target as the historical validation event. This event definition is appropriate for the 2012-2024 sample because first validation marked the transition from public commitment to third-party conformity assessment under the then-applicable SBTi framework. Subsequent SBTi developments, including expanded target-status logic and sector-specific standards, are therefore treated as interpretive context and robustness metadata rather than as variables that redefine historical treatment timing.

## 3. Scope 3 Variable Caveat

The Scope 3 indicator used in the legacy analysis is intentionally narrow: it captures firms with a standalone absolute Scope 3 target validated on or before the primary near-term validation event. It does not capture combined `1+2+3` targets or newer forms of Scope 3 treatment such as engagement, intensity, no-deforestation, or portfolio-alignment targets. The variable should therefore be interpreted as a historically precise marker of one form of value-chain ambition, not as a complete measure of Scope 3 exposure under the current SBTi taxonomy.

## 4. Financial Institutions Caveat

Financial institutions are retained in the historical baseline to preserve the original sample and avoid retroactive reclassification. However, the emergence of a distinct Financial Institutions Net-Zero Standard means that finance-sector firms now sit within a structurally different target-setting architecture. Results that pool financial and non-financial firms should therefore be interpreted as historical baseline estimates, with finance-excluded specifications used as sensitivity checks.

## 5. FLAG/Automotive Sector Caveat

Firms in FLAG-relevant and automotive sectors are retained in the historical baseline, but current SBTi guidance indicates that these sectors face increasingly distinct target-setting requirements. The updated robustness checks therefore flag these firms and test whether conclusions are sensitive to their exclusion. These flags do not imply that historical targets were invalid; they indicate that cross-sector comparability has become more complex.

## 6. Updated Limitations Paragraph

Several limitations remain. First, the research design is a within-adopter firm and year fixed-effects design rather than a canonical difference-in-differences design with a never-treated control group. Second, the updated SBTi dashboard fields used in robustness checks are current metadata, not historical event-time statuses. Third, the financial panel ends in 2024, so the analysis cannot assess post-2024 outcomes or the financial effects of later SBTi standards changes. Fourth, some dynamic event-study findings are sensitive to dummy construction and model specification, especially log revenue and employment. Finally, the analysis tests many outcomes and subgroups, so heterogeneity results should be interpreted as structured evidence rather than standalone causal proof.

## 7. Updated Future Research Paragraph

Future research should extend the financial panel beyond 2024, construct a matched non-SBTi control group, and incorporate historical SBTi tracker snapshots to distinguish initial validation, renewal validation, target updates, removals, and five-year review outcomes. A future-ready design should also develop method-aware ambition measures that account for absolute contraction, sectoral pathways, FLAG rules, financial-institution portfolio targets, and richer Scope 3 target types. Linking financial outcomes to observed emissions trajectories would further clarify whether the financial patterns identified here reflect actual transition performance or ex-ante firm readiness.

## 8. Updated Interpretation Of The Ambition Shield

The Ambition Shield should not be read simply as evidence that more demanding targets are cheaper to implement. A more defensible interpretation is that firms able to set more ambitious S1+S2 targets under the earlier SBTi framework may also have possessed stronger transition capacity, cleaner asset bases, better operational governance, or more credible decarbonization planning. The positive ambition interaction is therefore consistent with ambition moderating costs, but it may also reflect selection into ambitious target-setting by better-prepared firms.

## 9. Updated Interpretation Of The Scope 3 Puzzle

The Scope 3 Puzzle should be framed as a historical finding about firms with standalone absolute Scope 3 targets, not as evidence that Scope 3 decarbonization is financially easier. The updated SBTi taxonomy shows that Scope 3 target-setting now includes a broader range of target types and sector-specific mechanisms. The lower observed harm among legacy Scope 3 adopters is more plausibly interpreted as evidence that these firms were unusually mature in value-chain governance or supplier engagement when they entered the validation process.

## 10. Updated Interpretation Of Validation

First validation remains a useful historical event because it marks a firm's successful conformity assessment under SBTi criteria. However, validation should now be described as the start of a formal accountability cycle rather than the endpoint of credibility. Current SBTi practice places greater emphasis on target maintenance, review, renewal, status changes, and ongoing progress claims.

## 11. Updated Wording For SBTi As De Facto Standard/Substantive Commitment

The SBTi remains a central voluntary standard for corporate climate target-setting, and validation remains a credible signal of formal target conformity. At the same time, the initiative's evolution toward sector-specific, scope-specific, and lifecycle-based standards means that "substantive commitment" should be understood as conformity with a changing institutional framework rather than as a static binary label.

## 12. Updated Causal-Language Caveat

The results identify within-firm changes among SBTi adopters around commitment and validation events, conditional on firm fixed effects, year fixed effects, controls, and firm-clustered standard errors. They should be described as evidence consistent with the proposed mechanisms rather than as canonical difference-in-differences estimates against an untreated non-adopter control group.
